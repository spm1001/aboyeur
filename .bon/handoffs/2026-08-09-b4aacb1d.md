## For Claudes to come

Native cross-session messaging's fabric is filesystem-scoped, and that one fact organises everything: **sockets are the machine-wide roster** (every session binds `<pid>.sock` in a shared dir — `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/cc-socks` on Linux, `/tmp/cc-socks` on macOS), while **registry records are per-config-dir** (`$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR/sessions/<pid>.json` — so a commis-homed session registers where a reader of `~/.claude/sessions` never looks). Any tool that reads one source misses sessions the other sees; sonner unions both. The records are richer than the docs let on — name, cwd, tmux coordinates, busy/idle status — and their gaps are meaningful: a record with no `messagingSocketPath` field is a *live but deaf* session (Vertex-billed), not a dead one.

The machine boundary Anthropic drew (cross-machine = reply-only, via their servers) dissolved under the estate's own transport: carrying the ring over ssh and delivering locally on the far side makes tube→Mac initiation a one-liner, indistinguishable from a local peer message to the receiver. The general pattern — vendor plumbing for the local fabric, estate transport for the gaps the vendor left — is worth reaching for before accepting any future "not possible across X" in vendor docs.

Two behavioural results worth keeping: **peer framing survives a cold spawn** (a message delivered to a just-spawned session's socket arrives in the user-prompt slot but wrapped as a peer message, and the woken Claude treats it as a colleague, not as its user — this is why sonner spawns-then-delivers rather than passing text as a prompt), and **the harness silently drops byte-identical repeats from the same sender while telling the sender success** — the one place native messaging reintroduces sonnette's delivered-but-not-received wound. Anything periodic must vary its text; sonner stamps by default so the loss is impossible rather than documented.

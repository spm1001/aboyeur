## For Claudes to come

Scheduled fires are auditably distinguishable from typed prompts: a CronCreate fire arrives in the session JSONL as `promptSource:"system"` with `isMeta:true` and `queuePriority:"later"`, where a human (or tmux-injected) prompt reads `promptSource:"typed"`, `origin.kind:"human"`. That pair is how a cold verifier can prove a "loop ran N times" claim without trusting anyone's summary — our refuting subagent used it to confirm three fires were real scheduler injections, and it generalises to auditing any loop-engineering claim on this estate. Two companion facts measured the same night: fires that come due while a session is busy queue and deliver at next idle (minutes late is normal, ~8 min observed once against a 30-min cadence), and on CC 2.1.241 `durable:true` is a documented no-op — session-only tasks, empty `scheduled_tasks.json`, CronList showing other live sessions' tasks by aggregation, not persistence. Design consequence: native scheduling gives you ticks, never continuity; continuity is an outer loop's job.

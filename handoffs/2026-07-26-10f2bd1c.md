## For Claudes to come

**`claude -p` is a different product surface, not a cheaper layer of the interactive one.** CC names the split itself — `CLAUDE_CODE_ENTRYPOINT` reads `sdk-cli` headless and `cli` interactive — and inbound mesh tags, trust and permission dialogs, the statusline and session-start rituals exist *only* in the TUI. A headless green about interactive behaviour is an unearned green in its most convincing costume: the check ran, it passed, and the failure mode was structurally unable to appear. This estate re-derived the tmux workaround twice in one week before anyone wrote it down; the remedy is now a tool — the **`hublot`** skill in trousse (`start` / `wait`-for-pattern / `read` / `stop`), which absorbs the three traps that cost the time: a shell function like `claudem` needs a *genuinely interactive* shell (`bash -lc` returns early when non-interactive; `env -u` cannot exec a function at all), launching in an untrusted cwd writes a durable trust entry into the user's config as a side effect of a test, and `sleep`-and-hope is flaky and slow at once. It is orthogonal to ardoise and composes with it: ardoise isolates *what a session knows*, hublot drives *what a session does*.

The generalisation beyond CC, which is the part worth carrying: **when a probe returns nothing interesting, ask what would have had to happen for the answer to be present before concluding anything about the subject.** Tonight that question turned two dead ends into findings — a mistimed capability read became a clean recordable negative once re-timed, and an impossible headless test became a confirmed true positive once driven through a real TUI. The fix was a better instrument, not a better theory, both times.
